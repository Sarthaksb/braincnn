SLIDE 2: PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:
Problem Statement

Bullet Points:
• High Fatality & Critical Disease:
  Brain tumors are aggressive and require rapid, accurate clinical intervention.
• Manual MRI Analysis is Time-Consuming:
  Doctors and radiologists must examine hundreds of scan slices per patient, leading to diagnostic delays.
• High Risk of Human Error:
  Tumor shapes, boundaries, and textures vary greatly, making visual inspection complex.
• Shortage of Specialists:
  Many hospitals and rural clinics lack specialized neuro-radiologists.
• Need for Multi-Class Classification:
  Detecting "Tumor vs. No Tumor" is not enough; doctors require exact identification of the tumor type.


SLIDE 3: PROJECT OBJECTIVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:
Project Objectives

Bullet Points:
• Automated Diagnosis:
  Develop an automated computer-aided system to analyze Brain MRI scans in under 1 second.
• Multi-Class Tumor Classification:
  Accurately categorize scans into 4 classes: Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary, and Healthy Brain (No Tumor).
• High-Accuracy Deep Learning:
  Utilize Transfer Learning with DenseNet201 to eliminate vanishing gradients and maximize feature reuse.
• Clinical Decision Support Web App:
  Build a clean, accessible Streamlit web application for real-time MRI upload and testing.
• Explainable Confidence Breakdown:
  Provide clear prediction percentages across all four classes to assist doctors with reliable second opinions.


SLIDE 4: DATASET OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:
Dataset Overview

Bullet Points:
• Benchmark Dataset:
  Brain Tumor Classification (MRI) dataset created by Sartaj Bhuvaji et al. (Kaggle).
• Total Volume:
  3,264 high-resolution MRI scans organized into Training (2,870) and Testing (394) splits.
• Target Classes & Distribution:
  1. Glioma Tumor (926 images) – Malignant cancer originating in brain glial cells.
  2. Meningioma Tumor (937 images) – Slow-growing, mostly benign tumor on brain membranes.
  3. Pituitary Tumor (901 images) – Base skull tumor affecting the endocrine system.
  4. No Tumor (500 images) – Healthy normal brain scans used as a control group.
• Image Preprocessing:
  Resized to 224 x 224 pixels, normalized to [0, 1], with real-time zoom, shear, and flip augmentation.

SLIDE 6: SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title:
System Architecture

Subtitle / Note:
[Insert the generated diagram: "system_architecture.png" in the center of this slide]

Key Architecture Highlights:
• Stage 1 (Input): Patient brain MRI scan image (224 x 224 x 3).
• Stage 2 (Preprocessing): Pixel scaling (1/255) and data augmentation (shear, zoom, flips).
• Stage 3 (Feature Extraction): Pre-trained DenseNet201 backbone with direct layer-to-layer connections.
• Stage 4 (Custom Classifier Head):
  - Flatten Layer (spatial to 1D vector).
  - Dense Layer (256 units, ReLU activation).
  - Dropout Layer (0.3 rate to eliminate overfitting).
  - Dense Output Layer (4 units, Softmax activation).
• Stage 5 (Output & Deployment): Real-time diagnostic class, confidence percentage, and interactive Streamlit UI.
